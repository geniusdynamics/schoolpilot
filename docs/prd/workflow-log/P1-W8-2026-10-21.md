### What these two migrations got right

- **They refuse rather than guess.** M4 writes no grading scale — the legacy data has none — and keeps letters/remarks as rubric levels, ABS/EXM/INC as statuses with no score, and a dash as `incomplete` with a warning. M5 carries a foreign-currency payment in its own currency and reports it instead of converting at an invented rate, and writes an over-allocation only up to the amount actually paid.
- **They write their documents once, in their final state.** No `UPDATE` exists in M5, so the canonical posting freeze is respected by construction and a legacy draft stays a draft. The fixture drives the real repository: editing a migrated posted invoice raises `finance.posted_immutable`, and the migrated draft still edits.
- **They report every row they cannot map**, with the entity it would have become, so `before = after + unmapped` holds for every entity and nothing is dropped silently.
- **They roll back exactly what they created**, leaving M1–M3 and the legacy tables untouched (tested with foreign keys enforced and with a re-run after rollback).
- **M4 writes `learning_area` and `component`** because `mark.learning_area_id` is required and no earlier migration creates them; M5 migrates opening balances because omitting them would silently drop money from the acceptance. Both are deviations with the reasoning on the record.
